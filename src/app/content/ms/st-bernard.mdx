The Circle Food Market,  known by different names throughout history, is the latest iteration of centuries of food shopping, trade and community in New Orleans.  It stands at the intersection of St Bernard Ave and N Clairborne Ave and overcame extreme weather and urban development to remain open.

Long before the European settlement of New Orleans, Indigenous peoples traded goods on the land. When the city was settled in 1718, two tribes - the Oumas and the Tchouchoumas - set up a trading spot at the location of what is the current day French market.

In the 1850s, the Circle Food Market, then called the Saint Bernard Market, was one of over 30 public markets managed by the city of New Orleans for people to come and buy all kinds of fruits, vegetables, fish, oysters, and various dry goods. However,  it didn’t look then as it does now -- it was a much simpler structure: a slate roof to provide cover held up by iron pillars.

The Great Depression hit the public markets hard, and in an effort to attract shoppers and revive the markets the Works Project Administration overhauled the market buildings, including the Saint Bernard Market’s simple slate roof. In 1931, local architect Sam Stone Jr. was hired by the WPA to renovate the Saint Bernard Market. He created the grand Spanish Revival building that still stands today. But the timing was bad, the economic downturn coupled with the rise of modern supermarkets, forced the city to dissolve the Public Market Department and to sell all of its markets to private buyers. The Saint Bernard Market opened as the Circle Food Store in 1938. Despite changes in ownership, hurricanes and closing it’s doors on various locations, the market, now called the Circle Food market, still stands today as a traditional grocery.
