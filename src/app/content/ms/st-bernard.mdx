Since before European settlement, Indigenous people were trading goods on the land of present day New Orleans. And when the city was settled in 1718, two tribes - the Oumas and the Tchouchoumas - set up a trading spot at the location of what is the current day French market.

In the 1850s, the Saint Bernard Market was one of over 30 public markets provided by the city for people to come and buy all kinds of fruits, vegetables, fish, oysters, and various dry goods. However,  it didn’t look then as it does now -- it was a much simpler structure: a slate roof to provide some cover held up by iron pillars.

The Great Depression hit the markets hard, and in an effort to [TODO], the Works Project Administration in the 1930s rennovated and built new buildings to revive the markets. But the timing was bad -- with the rise of the supermarket, the city eventually decided to sell all of its markets to private buyers and withdraw from running the markets themself.

The Saint Bernard Market was part of the 1930's WPA overhaul and hired Sam Stone Jr., a local architect, to create the grand Spanish Revival building that you see today.
