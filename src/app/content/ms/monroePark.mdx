During the early 1900s, Monroe Park was a unique entertainment destination for tourists and Mobile residents alike.  The turn of the century wonderland, located along Mobile Bay south of downtown, was typically reached by streetcar, and was unlike anything currently found in the Port City. 

<LOCItem type="pnp" image="/loc/ms/monroePark/streetcar.jpg" alt="Black and white photograph of a streetcar on St. Charles Avenue, New Orleans, Louisiana." caption="Photograph of a streetcar, St. Charles Avenue, New Orleans, Louisiana" linkOut="https://www.loc.gov/resource/highsm.04876/"/>


After disembarking the trolley, visitors would stroll bench-lined walking paths shaded by a canopy of oaks, cypress, and other hardy trees. Besides being home to the Mobile Light and Railway Co., Monroe Park boasted a baseball stadium where now-legendary players like Babe Ruth competed in exhibition games, a picturesque yacht club where locals gathered, and the latest in electrical entertainment such as a: penny arcade, carousel, outdoor movie theater, and roller coaster.
<LOCItem type="pnp" image="/loc/ms/monroePark/carousel.jpg" alt="Black and white photograph of the Carousel at Monroe Park, Mobile, Alabama." caption="Photograph of the carousel at Monroe Park between 1900-1915." linkOut="https://www.loc.gov/resource/det.4a19818/"/>

Situated not far south from present day Arlington Park, current day Monroe Park bears little resemblance to the entertainment complex of the past. Today's manicured walking park is flanked by a reedy shoreline and crowned with a rustic fishing pier and humble kayak launch. Bird calls fill the air and an alligator might prowl the seagrass as cargo ships, cruise liners, and other watercraft steam by. Today, nature is the star, but over a 120 years ago, the tinny sounds of the musical
carousel or thrill-seeking riders taking the plunge on the clattering wooden roller coaster would have drowned out any natural sounds.

The roller coaster unfortunately did not survive the 1906 hurricane that struck the Gulf Coast that September. Young lovers often strolled the Yacht Club pier so they could take shelter in the shade of the George B. Rogers designed clubhouse at the end of the structure. The July 1916 hurricane destroyed that masterpiece, but fortunately you can see other architectural legacies by George B. Rogers still standing from downtown Mobile to Bellingrath Gardens & Home in south Mobile County.

After the ravages of intense hurricanes during the early twentieth century, including a third one in 1926, Mobile’s bayfront area switched from entertaining pleasure seekers to a focus on national defense. The Brookley Army Air Field ruled the location from 1938 until 1969. After a period of decline, what is known to locals as the Brookley Complex is enjoying a season of revitalization. The next advancement, beyond the new terminal for the Mobile International Airport less than a mile inland, is reclaiming more public space along the water for a park called “Brookley By the Bay.” This new area south of Arlington Park is planned to feature not only water access for swimming, but walking and biking trails, playgrounds, areas for sports, and an amphitheater—bringing waterfront entertainment back to Monroe Park’s old footprint.
