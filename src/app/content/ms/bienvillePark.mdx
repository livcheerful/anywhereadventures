Known as Bienville Park in 1906, a swath of green in the heart of downtown Mobile, Bienville Square was established by the US Congress in 1824 just five years after Alabama became a state and is one of the oldest public squares in the United States. 

Grazing livestock, specifically pigs and cattle, and hanging laundry in the community space became daily occurrences in the 1840s. In 1905, President Theodore Roosevelt gave a speech in the park about the importance of the Panama Canal, that construction had begun on the previous year, to Mobile’s port and shipping industry.

Bienville Square is home to the stately Ketchum fountain constructed of cast iron in 1857 and lined with massive live oaks that provide cool, shaded respites for those relaxing on benches and of course, plenty of acorns. Locals and visitors alike stop by A&M Peanut Shop across Dauphin Street for a bag of roasted nuts to share with the friendly squirrels. These squirrels are so well-known and adored, there’s even a Mardi Gras organization, the Squirrels of Bienville, who join the People’s Parade on Joe Cain Day.
