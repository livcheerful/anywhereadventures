Before they had the right to vote, women were finding employment in different ways. In the US government, one of the first jobs available to women was the unique position of lighthouse keeper. It is unique in its responsibilities - because the keeper will live on-site to maintain and run the lighthouse, entire families’ lives would anchor around these seaside structures. The Biloxi Lighthouse is notable for being maintained by women the longest of any other lighthouse in the US.

Mary Reynolds, keeper from 1854 - 1866 was appointed to be the second keeper of the Biloxi Lighthouse. She even kept it shining through the "destructive" 1860 hurricane where she said others "stood appalled at the danger I encountered". However, she found herself sitting in historic times. When the Civil War began in 1861, lighthouses were extinguished to prevent boats from safely navigating the water. She wrote a letter to the governor trying to appeal the loss of her only source of income, but the keys to the lighthouse were taken by a group of citizens. She noted that there was a large amount of oil still in the tower, and she had “seen disreputable characters taking out oil in bottles”. (She continued her career as lighthouse keeper in nearby Pass Christian)

In 1866, Perry Younghans was appointed the new keeper, bringing his wife, Maria, and daughter, Miranda, along with him. A short year later, he died, and the widowed Maria assumed responsibility for the lighthouse.

The lighthouse was a family affair: though Maria was officially keeper until 1918, her daughter, Miranda, helped her with her duties. When Maria retired, Miranda became the keeper.

<LOCItem type="pnp" image="/loc/ms/lighthouse/lighthouse.jpg" linkOut="https://www.loc.gov/item/2016802914/" caption="Biloxi Ligthouse in 1901, when Miranda Reynolds was keeper"/>

In 1926, the Biloxi keeper once again found herself writing a letter to keep her job. Since Miranda was reaching the age of 72, the scheduled day of her retirement was to be April 24, 1927. She wrote a letter to Superintendent of Lighthouses, E.S. Lanphier, asking that he extend her appointment so that she can get her finances in order. He forwarded her letter to the Commissioner of Lighthouses, and it was approved that she get her extension until April 23, 1929. This ended the long period of 62 years when the Biloxi Lighthouse was maintained by women.
