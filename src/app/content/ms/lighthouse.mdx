One of the first federal jobs available to women was the position of lighthouse keeper. The role is unique in its responsibilities - because the keeper would live onsite to maintain and run the lighthouse and family life would revolve around these seaside structures. The Biloxi Lighthouse is especially notable because it has been maintend by women keepers for more years than any other lighthouse in the US.

Mary Reynolds, keeper from 1854 - 1866 was appointed as the second keeper of the Biloxi Lighthouse. She even kept it shining through the destructive 1860 hurricane where she said others "stood appalled at the danger I encountered". However, she soon found herself in the middle of another historic event. The Civil War began in 1861, and lighthouses were extinguished to prevent enemy boats from safely navigating the sea. She wrote a letter to the governor appealing the loss of her only source of income, but the keys to the lighthouse were taken away by a group of citizens. She noted that there was a large amount of oil still in the tower, and she that she had “seen disreputable characters taking out oil in bottles”. (She continued her career as lighthouse keeper in nearby Pass Christian)

In 1866 after the war, Perry Younghans was appointed as the new keeper, bringing his wife, Maria, and daughter, Miranda, along with him. A short year later, he died, and the widowed Maria assumed responsibility for the lighthouse.

Maintaing the lighthouse was a family affair: though Maria was officially keeper until 1918, her daughter, Miranda, helped her with her duties. When Maria retired, Miranda became the keeper.

In 1926, the Biloxi keeper once again found herself writing a letter to keep her job. Since Miranda was reaching the age of 72, her retirement was scheduled to begin on April 24, 1927. She wrote a letter to the Superintendent of Lighthouses, E.S. Lanphier, asking to extend her appointment so that she could get her finances in order. He forwarded her letter to the Commissioner of Lighthouses, and a two-year extension was approved. With her retirement on April 23, 1929, the 62 year run of women keepers at the Biloxi Lighthouse ended.
