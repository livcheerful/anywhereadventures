However, there was a simple solution using a simple machine: building a series of funiculars to steadily carry both people and freight up and into the hills. Funiculars, also known as inclines, utilize one of the six simple machines from physics to efficiently move large amounts of weight from one height to another. This solution was so good for the city that 17 were built over the course of over 40 years, rimming the perimeter.

For a long time, the inclines were the only way to get from South Shore to Mount Washington and would carry things ranging from passengers to horse-drawn wagons.

When the McArdle Roadway opened, the new path made the slower inclines obsolete. Over time, almost all the inclines fell out of use. Now, only two remain: the original incline, the Monogahela, and the nearby Duquesne. They are still operating as a place for fun and to relieve your legs from an exhausting hill climb.
