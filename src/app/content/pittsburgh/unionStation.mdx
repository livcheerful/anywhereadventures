## **Nope!**

Pittsburgh was named after William Pitt, and “burgh” is a shortening of the word “borough” (which is a word from Old English burg or burh, meaning “fortified settlement”). Most places in the US spell it as “burg” (sans H), such as Schaumburg, Williamsburg, and Ellensburg. 

General John Forbes, a British army leader who captured French Fort Duquesne which would become Pittsburgh, penned the first letter with the Pittsburgh name in 1758.

In the 1890s, the federal government began an effort to standardize place names across the country. They developed a set of naming conventions, such as changing “-centre” to “-center”, “-borough” to “-boro”, and of course, “-burgh” to “-burg”.

But that’s not how it shook out for Pittsbugh! The city had so much momentum in its original name - many local organizations had established themselves with the original name, such as the Pittsburgh Gazette and the University of Pittsburgh. No matter how much the federal government pushed, the local community continued on with the historical sticky “H” spelling.

Finally in 1911, an exception for the “burg” rule was made, and the name “Pittsburgh” was recognized nationwide.

(However, there were still some holdouts for the Pittsburg name from organizations who named themselves after the federal standard and didn’t want to adopt the local "h")
